Internal message · Marked for management review

Low attendance does not always mean low need

FromAndre Wilson · Vauxhall Branch ManagerToCommunity Program Review TeamDateNovember 9, 2026 · 10:12 AM

I heard that smaller programs may be reduced so we can move staff and space toward the sessions with the largest attendance. I understand the pressure to serve more residents. But I am worried we are using the wrong definition of success.

Some residents learn about programs through conversations at the front desk, a neighborhood flyer, or a trusted community group—not through the online calendar. A small group of regular participants may rely on the library for language practice, confidence, and connection. If we count only registered attendance, we may end up eliminating services that help people least likely to be represented in the registration system.

Before we call a program unpopular, we should ask whether residents could find it, reach it, and understand that it was meant for them.

Reply · Another perspective

Re: Low attendance does not always mean low need

FromMaya Bennett · Community Programs DirectorToCommunity Program Review TeamDateNovember 9, 2026 · 10:47 AM

Andre makes an important point. But we also have limited rooms, facilitators, and staff time. Keeping every program unchanged is not fair to people waiting for other services. We need a process for deciding what counts as reach, accessibility, and community value—not a rule that automatically favors either small or large programs.
