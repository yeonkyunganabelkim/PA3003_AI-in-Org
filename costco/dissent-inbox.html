We cannot forecast our way out of an unfinished handoff

FromMarcus Reed · Merchandising & Floor Operations SupervisorToWarehouse Improvement Task ForceDateNovember 9, 2026 · 8:14 AM

Yesterday, a member asked why a frequently purchased household item was missing from the shelf. The first response was that we might have sold out. Receiving had logged the product earlier. But the merchandise had not made it from the back area to the floor, and the associate who usually helps with replenishment had been reassigned to cover another station. The member left before anyone could give a reliable answer.

I keep hearing that a better AI demand forecast will fix availability. Maybe forecasting would help some products. But if the product is physically in our building and our teams cannot move it to the shelf or confirm its location, what exactly are we forecasting? Staff have raised the handoff problem more than once. A few have stopped bringing it up because they assume the decision has already been made.

Please talk with receiving and floor teams before buying anything. An empty shelf is visible; the work that created it usually is not.

Re: We cannot forecast our way out of an unfinished handoff

FromPriya Shah · Inventory Planning ManagerToWarehouse Improvement Task ForceDateNovember 9, 2026 · 9:03 AM

Marcus is right that handoffs deserve scrutiny, but we should not treat one observed incident as proof that every empty shelf is a replenishment failure. Some products may truly be unavailable, and an on-hand record can itself be wrong. We need to distinguish inventory accuracy, staffing, and floor execution before choosing the problem.
