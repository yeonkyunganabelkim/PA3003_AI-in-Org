The station knew. The passengers did not.

FromJanelle Brooks · Station Services & Accessibility LeadToPassenger Information Review TeamDateNovember 9, 2026 · 7:35 AM

During a recent service disruption, station employees could see that normal operations had changed. Riders were already gathering at the platform edge and asking whether to wait, move, or find another route. We did not have a verified recovery time or an approved message explaining what was happening.

People keep calling this a slow-app problem. But a faster app cannot publish information that no one has permission to release. Meanwhile, passengers who rely on station announcements or accessible guidance may receive even less useful information. Staff are placed in an impossible position: remain silent and appear indifferent, or speak too early and risk giving inaccurate directions.

Can we please investigate who is authorized to communicate uncertainty, not just how quickly the software sends alerts?

Reply · Another perspective

Re: The station knew. The passengers did not.

FromOwen Gallagher · Regional Rail Operations ManagerToPassenger Information Review TeamDateNovember 9, 2026 · 8:07 AM

Unverified guidance can cause riders to move to the wrong platform or choose an unsafe alternative. The team needs to separate necessary safety verification from avoidable communication delay.
